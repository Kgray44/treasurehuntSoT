## Index posture and screen-edge handling

Main required seven joints `[0,5,6,7,8,9,17]`: wrist, index and a palm span. It also rejected any required point within 1.5% of an image border. The new model-based posture requires the four observed index joints **`[5,6,7,8]`**. A wrist, middle/little knuckle or palm width is no longer needed. The extra 1.5% inset is removed for these required points; finite index coordinates must still be inside `[0,1]`. A missing index joint is not manufactured from off-image estimates.

Index geometry corrects normalized y by `frameHeight/frameWidth` and uses inferred relative z. This prevents a camera-facing finger's short 2D projection alone from making it appear folded. It does not provide metric depth, a measured screen ray or reliable knowledge of a hidden joint. The model has no per-joint visibility confidence: a finite in-image estimate is not proof that a joint was visibly resolved or that a digit was labelled correctly.

| Current index anatomy test                    | Value                                                                                                   |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Complete required index chain                 | Four finite, valid in-image joints                                                                      |
| Individual segment for anatomical eligibility | At least `0.001` in aspect-correct normalized geometry                                                  |
| Total chain for posture geometry              | `0.012` through `1.2`                                                                                   |
| Middle/proximal segment ratio                 | `0.22` through `1.4`                                                                                    |
| Distal/proximal segment ratio                 | `0.18` through `1.2`                                                                                    |
| Extended index: chord/chain                   | At least `0.78`                                                                                         |
| Extended index: PIP/DIP alignment cosines     | At least `0.2` / `0`                                                                                    |
| Marginal diagnostic evidence                  | Chord/chain at least `0.70`, both joint cosines at least `-0.1`; this is not fresh pointing eligibility |

A relaxed or slightly bent index can therefore qualify without a fist. Cropped or depth-overlapping other fingers cannot veto the index merely because the model guesses they are open. A secondary finger supplies contrary evidence only with a complete, separated projected chain: each projected bone at least three pixels, projected reach at least 12 pixels, tip separation from the index at least eight pixels, projected reach/3D chain at least `0.5`, and projected straightness at least `0.85`. These pixel limits use the source frame width after aspect correction.

Legible open, parallel competing or pinching anatomy still blocks model pointing. Two clearly open secondary fingers require straightness at least `0.93` and PIP/DIP cosines at least `0.7`; a competing finger uses the stricter `0.96` straightness and `0.85` joint cosines plus directional tests. A valid thumb tip within `0.12` index-chain lengths of the index tip is a pinch veto. Curled, hooked, collapsed, excessive or nonfinite index geometry remains ineligible.

The UI distinguishes **index joints tracked**, **heuristic index-only intent**, and evidence compared with visible fingers. The projected-finger rules above describe the index-led fallback posture classifier; coherent whole-hand context has the separate priority described below. Absence of other fingers does not prove intent or a confidence percentage. Bounded off-image secondary estimates remain explicitly auxiliary and cannot rescue the required index or drive its cursor coordinates.

Full-hand gesture evidence has priority over the fallback. Availability requires observed palm/knuckle and non-index anatomy, not a landmark count alone, and does not require the wrist. A coherent full hand authorizes pointing only with compatible index/image evidence. Open or bent posture, full-hand ambiguity or index disagreement pauses collection; manual image hints cannot bypass that decision. With no available full hand, index/image evidence can supply the fallback. Image coordinates remain the relative source across this arbitration, so detection priority does not silently switch calibration vectors or snap the cursor. Completed targets remain saved through temporary pauses.

Full-hand promotion and a persistent contrary latch require at least **three compatible fresh frames spanning 120 ms**, with no inter-frame gap above **250 ms**. Evidence belongs to one model track and camera context; source changes, gaps, disagreement, a new image selection or expiry clear the candidate. A coherent negative pauses pointing immediately, but an isolated or spatially conflicting return cannot create a persistent negative latch. A confirmed negative needs compatible confirmed full pointing again, or an explicit new hint when full-hand authority is unavailable. Image coordinates never rebind during this handoff.

A fresh optical match during a gesture pause is shown as a **gray dashed ring**, labelled “Fingertip image matched · pointing paused.” Its separate `matchedPoint` metadata is normalized, source/freshness gated and display-only. The actionable `point` remains null; the gray marker cannot enter relative features, collection, independent validation or playground actions. The gold ring denotes eligible image pointing. Both disappear when their observations become stale.

## Independent image continuity

The new `watchglass.index-image` channel tracks a selected local appearance patch using **bounded photometric normalized cross-correlation**. A model index can initialize it from the same camera capture. When only a finger is available, **Track visible fingertip** lets the person mark the tip with a click/tap or arrows plus Enter; Escape cancels. Mirrored and natural previews map the selection into the original image coordinates. A manual reseed always starts a new image generation.

This is an appearance tracker, not an automatic finger classifier. A manual hint is human identification of a visible point. A queued hint still needs a suitable image match, and the hint control waits for the first fresh processed image. Texture, blur, repeated patterns, clipping and large motion can make it abstain. A wrong model result cannot pull an established good image position to another digit.

Explicit manual selection now remains reserved after an unsuitable initial image or the 1,200 ms image-track expiry. It cannot silently return to automatic model seeding. A hint gets one source-image attempt and a 150 ms dispatch-age bound; its old x/y is not retried after that attempt. Failed acquisition or expiry asks for a new mark. **Use automatic fingertip selection** is a separate explicit action; it clears the old image binding and fit with a visible reason while leaving the camera running. A deliberate new manual mark also clears any earlier fit immediately, so a failed mark cannot leave the old source active. These changes do not make an image hint override available whole-hand gesture authority.

Whole-hand gesture authority requires a plausible observed knuckle row `[5,9,13,17]` and at least two anatomically coherent complete other fingers; a wrist is not required. Palm span must be `0.035` through `0.65`; the two intermediate knuckles must lie from `0.05` through `0.95` along that span, be separated by at least `0.05` and have arch ratio at most `0.65`. Complete finger chain lengths must be `0.45` through `3.5` palm spans, with nondegenerate segments and the same bounded segment ratios. An in-bounds joint count alone cannot establish this context.

- A coherent full-hand point must agree with the image tip within eight analysis pixels. Temporal promotion needs at least three compatible frames spanning 120 ms, with no gap over 250 ms and the same exact model track/context. The image continues to supply the coordinates and identity.
- A full-hand pointing result at a conflicting location, missing/ambiguous index anatomy or two credible whole hands pauses output. It does not snap or reanchor the image point. Ambiguity and location conflict can recover on a later compatible frame or fallback when the whole hand becomes unavailable.
- A coherent full-hand open/folded/pinched or competing pose immediately pauses action. A spatially disagreeing return is a frame-local conflict, never a persistent contrary latch. A matching negative becomes latched only after the same three-frame/120 ms evidence requirement; that confirmed decision survives subsequent no-hand or partial-index frames until a compatible full-hand point is also temporally confirmed. A manual hint while whole-hand authority remains visible cannot bypass it.
- A deliberate new hint can clear the old contrary latch only when whole-hand authority is unavailable. It creates a new source identity and fresh setup rather than rebinding saved calibration.

During a gesture pause, a fresh accepted image match may continue as a gray dashed preview. Its `matchedPoint` is display-only; the actionable point remains null, and no calibration sample or action uses the preview. Optical failure, occlusion or stale capture removes that preview too. A single bad model return can therefore recover naturally through image fallback without changing image identity or restarting setup.

## Smoothing, freshness and adaptive scheduling

Display smoothing now filters signed velocity before increasing the cutoff during deliberate motion. Its base cutoff is 1.5 Hz, increases by three times filtered speed and is capped at 12 Hz; velocity is filtered at 1 Hz. A single unexpected jump waits for a coherent confirming observation. This changes display behavior only. A held display point expires after **150 ms** from its last accepted estimate; a faded dashed **Last seen** marker expires after **450 ms** from that same timestamp. Rejected input cannot refresh either or enter calibration/practice actions.

Main used a fixed 40 ms scheduling delay around worker completion. The adaptive runtime now admits only a genuinely presented video frame, using `requestVideoFrameCallback` or a playback-quality presented-frame counter fallback. A moving video playback clock alone is not fresh image evidence. Normal focused tracking defaults to **15 Hz**. An active, unpaused **TRAINING** or **VALIDATION** session raises the phase ceiling to **60 Hz**; pausing or returning to PREPARE, RESULTS or PLAYGROUND restores the normal ceiling. Visible unfocused work remains capped at **8 Hz**. The separate model ceiling defaults to **60 Hz**; effective scheduling uses the lower applicable phase/model ceilings. Available camera frames, processing pressure and bounded rate recovery can reduce every ceiling further. Hidden pages stop capture.

The scheduler budgets measured main-thread CV work to **30%**; worker elapsed time no longer adds the former 25% idle period. These are local scheduling budgets, not measured whole-system CPU percentages. Late timers and expensive frames reduce work promptly; recovery increases rate by at most 10% per completion. Image processing is disabled for the model-only absolute path. One in-flight frame and bounded buffers prevent a stale backlog. None of this establishes exposure-to-display latency.

The higher calibration ceiling preserves that bounded recovery; it does not force an immediate jump to 60 updates/second or bypass one-in-flight backpressure. Historical browser receipts retain the policy of their exact source, including earlier focused 60 Hz and normal 30 Hz ceilings; they are not measurements of the current 15/60 Hz defaults.

The renderer still polls at the display animation cadence, but submits canvas commands only for changed inputs, resize/DPR changes, the 150/450 ms freshness transitions, or an active last-seen fade. Rendering geometry, colors and every fading frame are preserved. A mocked-renderer check with 60 animation ticks and five source updates submitted ten camera drawings (five fresh and five dim), rather than 60 identical submissions. This is a draw-count check, not measured GPU utilization or physical frame rate. Canvas timing now records actual submissions rather than repeated draws of the same capture; its P95 is not directly comparable with the older repeated-RAF metric.

1. Start with **Relative movement**. Enable the camera, keep a comfortable index visible and allow image tracking to settle. If the model cannot initialize a finger-only view, choose **Track visible fingertip** and mark the actual tip. The gold ring follows the image-selected point; it is not a 3D confidence marker.
2. Move bottom-to-top and through both lower corners with relaxed fingers. Alternate a full hand and finger-only view, briefly occlude, and watch for an explicit pause/recovery rather than a jump to another digit. A visible full-hand non-pointing pose or a model/image location conflict should pause output. Restore a compatible full-hand point; a fresh manual hint is a recovery option only when that whole-hand context is unavailable. Poor texture or severe blur may honestly pause tracking.
3. Begin relative setup. During an unfinished hold, briefly occlude or relax and confirm completed targets remain saved. Use Pause/Resume once. A deliberate new fingertip selection must require a fresh setup.
4. Complete the five independent checks. If the raw P95 exceeds 3%, read the measured result and retry deliberately; do not treat a completed check as a passing check.
5. Test **Absolute pointing** separately if desired. It needs the full observed four-joint model index and a separate nine-target fit. Camera-facing relative z remains inferred; the webcam may not achieve the accuracy limit in every pose or location.
6. Use the recorder only when a local diagnostic capture is wanted. Confirm its recording consent and download both files before leaving. Reporting a failure with its target, pose and displayed pause/source reason is more useful than changing the independent accuracy requirement.
