## Tracker and model

The hand recognizer remains MediaPipe Hand Landmarker, pinned float16 task version 1, runtime 0.10.32, with 21 joints and at most two hands. Model SHA-256 is `fbc2a30080c3c557093b5ddfc334698132eb341044ccee322ccf8bcf3607cde1`. Detection, presence and tracking thresholds remain 0.6. The optional current-region GPU worker and original CPU/WASM fallback retain the same weights. No replacement neural network is installed in Reach.

Current observation interpretation is adapter version `1.6.0` with palm-continuity policy v3. The relative image producer is version `4`, algorithm `TEMPORAL_INDEX_FUSION_V3`, descriptor digest `d44f622206af73179698c692871bbfc515ff074f48bacb038d86449b78d8c42a`. These describe interpretation and provenance; they do not identify new neural weights or certify pointing accuracy. The later calibration hold changes only the laboratory controller and leaves those producer bytes unchanged.

The image tracker is a custom normalized cross-correlation (NCC) matcher. It compares a selected 15 by 15 grayscale patch at an analysis dimension no greater than 320 pixels, using a stable reference and a recent adaptive patch, rival separation, texture/structure checks and reverse correspondence. It measures appearance; it cannot identify a finger from appearance alone. The hand model supplies index semantics and pointing intent. Image matching supplies local measured movement when its evidence is compatible. Neither inferred depth nor the reported uncertainty proxy establishes physical screen-ray accuracy.

A private feasibility comparison tried pyramidal sparse Lucas–Kanade tracking with two configurations on the owner's transition. Both rejected the blurred transition and failed to recover without reseeding. That is useful negative evidence against replacing NCC blindly; it does not establish that all optical-flow implementations would fail. Original failed receipts and private frames remain local.

## Timing and proof boundaries

Live inference and NCC use the same immutable bitmap and capture token. The saved diagnostic video is composed independently at 20 frames per second, while its trace samples the latest camera media time before bitmap capture. Nearest-frame video/trace alignment can therefore create apparent endpoint differences during fast motion. Offline paired results are diagnostic evidence and cannot justify widening live gates without a same-image check.

The normal, calibration, model and visible-unfocused ceilings remain 15, 60, 60 and 8 Hz. These are ceilings; actual frames, compute pressure and server policy can lower them. There remains one inference in flight, a 150 ms freshness bound and hidden-page resource release.

The independent five held-out checks, at least fifteen samples and 600 ms per hold, and raw P95 error no greater than 3% of the testing plane diagonal remain unchanged. Display smoothing cannot make a failed raw check pass. Full protected qualification, integration and fresh owner physical acceptance are outside this development audit.

## Temporal identity addendum

The successor introduces a narrowly owned `TemporalIndexTrack`. Its logical session identity is separate from a MediaPipe adapter generation and an NCC optical epoch. It retains the last accepted measurement and capture time, bounded image-space velocity, a conservative uncertainty proxy, source history and a short recovery history. The existing producer retains anatomical index references, appearance parts, negative gesture state and disagreement evidence. The laboratory's canonical controller retains calibration binding and saved target evidence; the tracker does not create a second calibration ledger.

The temporal states are `ACQUIRING`, `TRACKING`, `UNCERTAIN`, `COASTING`, `REACQUIRING`, `AMBIGUOUS` and `EXPIRED`. Position and intent are separate: a fresh selected image can have unknown current anatomical posture, and a clearly observed hand can retain association while its index coordinate remains unavailable. A raw template selection can be a logical candidate without being a usable pointer. A genuine open-hand decision remains negative even when its pixels cannot match. Conflicting candidates do not update trusted templates, trajectory, velocity or acceptance clocks.

Temporary model-ID aliases have a bounded probation: at most three current candidates, three fully supported frames spanning 120 ms, gaps at most 250 ms and trusted-reference age at most 1,200 ms. Only the final temporary track ID/generation may change; the first ten provider/camera/geometry provenance fields must match. Current fixed-reference anatomy, the old distinctive tip and two separated old index appearance regions including DIP must support a new alias. Unsupported foreign candidates are quarantined before they can mutate the trusted anatomy or skin reference. A previously proved exact adapter generation retains physical association and negative intent through posture uncertainty; it gains no new index-coordinate permission from that fact.

Automatic cold acquisition also has probation. The first neural index guess can prepare an image candidate, but image matching alone cannot promote it. Current coherent full-hand or registered-index geometry must satisfy the three-frame/120 ms requirement before it publishes a point. This addresses a recorded failure where one wrong neural seed was followed faithfully by the image tracker after neural detection disappeared.

The existing bounded constant-velocity predictor supplies diagnostic coast positions only. Its age includes processing completion, and it stops at 150 ms from the last measured capture. It never enters calibration, validation or actions. Both temporal and source-alias state roll back if final dispatch crosses freshness or loses its source. The normal display filter remains unchanged.
