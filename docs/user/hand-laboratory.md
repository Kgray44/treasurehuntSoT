# Find your point

Open **Device connections → Find the Hand interaction laboratory**. Reach lets you see your tracked hand, set up a pointer, check it independently and practice. Ordinary buttons, touch and keyboard controls remain available throughout.

The latest [selected-index repair](../../Development_Docs/Projects/Project_Reach/Selected_Index_Identity_Repair_20261018.md) is being validated after the owner still encountered calibration resets in the earlier preview, despite its passing software checks. Reliable physical use is not yet established.

Choose **Enable live camera** to allow local hand tracking. The standard laboratory requests no microphone, uploads no camera frames and records no video. Place the camera near your screen and sit comfortably. The hand model and its joint drawing remain visible separately from pointing eligibility and sample collection.

The [earlier index continuity record](../../Development_Docs/Projects/Project_Reach/Index_Finger_Continuity_20261017.md) introduced support for a coherent observed index chain when the wrist, palm and other fingers leave view. Relaxed fingers and a gently bent index are allowed. Clearly conflicting visible gestures still pause collection. Hidden fingers cannot prove intent, and monocular inferred depth does not establish a physical screen ray. An index aimed toward the camera can be recognized through joint relationships even when its projected length is short.

Relative movement combines current model index positions with local fingertip image motion. Compatible measurements receive a bounded weighted correction; a small persistent difference does not steadily pull the pointer all the way to the model estimate. A supported image endpoint can remain the position when the model estimates a shorter tip on the same finger. The [previous fused tracking audit](../../Development_Docs/Projects/Project_Reach/Fused_Tracking_Audit_20261018.md) preserves the earlier implementation and its results.

Keeping the same hand in view does not by itself verify the selected fingertip. If its position becomes uncertain, collection pauses and completed targets can stay saved. A returning index is compared with its previously verified reference after accounting for observed hand motion; another plausible-looking digit cannot simply replace that reference. A trustworthy current model or compatible image evidence is still required for movement. Image tracking can continue when the model returns no hand. The gold ring marks the resulting fingertip; the original hand drawing remains the model's output. A conflict pauses collection instead of jumping to another digit. Only current evidence supplies samples; predicted or held positions do not.

If the hand model cannot initialize because only a finger is visible, choose **Track visible fingertip** and click its tip in the camera preview. Keyboard users can move the crosshair with arrows and confirm with Enter; Escape cancels. This is an explicit appearance hint, not automatic finger recognition. Selecting a new fingertip starts a new track and requires fresh setup. Featureless, blurred, clipped or visually repetitive regions may not provide a safe match; show a little more of the finger or hand to reacquire. A finger cannot be followed once it is completely outside the camera image.

The full-hand model remains the source for experimental absolute pointing. Relative fused tracking and absolute model geometry use separate calibration profiles and cannot share a fit or samples. Earlier image-only calibrations also require a fresh setup. An owner-authorized private recording revealed lower-right model dropouts and occasional mislabelled folded fingers; its complete lower-target frames were reviewed. The owner's unperformed sweep is excluded from physical motion claims. This development preview still needs a fresh physical trial.

**Relative movement** is the recommended starting point. Set a comfortable center and four movement directions, then move your hand to guide the amber cursor to five new check targets. This checks cursor control; it does not establish where you physically point. **Absolute pointing** is experimental: point naturally at nine screen targets, then point at five distinct held-out targets. A fitted model remains unavailable for practice until its separate check passes. Ordinary RGB webcams do not determine physical screen intersection reliably for every posture or camera placement.

You can open setup once the camera/model session is available, then find a comfortable point at the first target. Opening setup collects nothing; only fresh, eligible tracking starts progress.

At each target, let your hand settle until the progress indicator fills. Brief tracking imperfections pause collection; completed targets stay saved. The development preview retains agreeing hold evidence for up to 2.5 seconds, without counting the interruption as evidence. An isolated posture or framing dip can resume on fresh eligible evidence from the same verified fingertip. Actual tracking loss, ambiguity or sustained ineligibility requires fresh settling. Model continuity uses a 120 ms recovery interval; image readiness buffers several fresh good frames and can take longer at a reduced capture rate. **Pause**, **Resume** and **Retry this target** let you rest or retry without starting over; there is no forced 20-second retry. Sustained loss expires only the unfinished hold while the same hand binding remains. A currently observed palm may preserve hand selection, but it cannot refresh an unverified fingertip position or authorize new samples. Camera, provider, mirror, orientation, view-size or deliberate fingertip-selection changes invalidate setup. Local model hand continuity lasts up to 1.2 seconds of absence; longer disappearance or an ambiguous crossing may create a new generation. Relative training/checks suspend their old evidence when continuity is unverified, as described below; absolute setup retains its stricter generation reset. Moving the pointing area on screen also invalidates live absolute calibration. Keep the screen and camera stationary; manually recalibrate after camera movement that tracking cannot observe.

Only fresh, eligible captures add samples. The cursor pauses when its latest observation becomes older than 150 milliseconds. That brief display pause alone does not erase agreeing hold progress between valid captures. A rejected result, actual loss of eligible pointing or **Pause** still interrupts evidence; time across that interruption is not counted. The independent accuracy check remains required.

A detected hand can still have no usable fingertip position. Appearance-related pauses now explain a mismatched, ambiguous or inconsistent fingertip image separately from a missing hand. Open **Session diagnostics & privacy** to read **Current tracking decision** when reporting a stall. Automatic recovery remains limited in this development preview; keeping a hand visible does not guarantee that a lost index image can be reidentified. Selecting a new fingertip still requires fresh setup.

A genuine reset leaves a persistent **Setup ended** alert with its reason and moves keyboard focus to the alert. The camera remains available so you can begin a fresh setup when ready.

In relative setup or its independent check, an unverified replacement tracking identity now keeps completed targets and the original binding in a suspended trial. **Saved evidence paused · identity unverified** means those saved samples cannot be used with the new track: collection, fitting, checking, pointer interaction and export remain blocked. Only fresh proof of the original logical fingertip can resume that trial. **Start fresh setup** deliberately discards the old evidence and begins at target one; **Cancel setup** returns to preparation. This preserves your progress for inspection without pretending that continuity was proved. Camera, provider, image geometry and deliberate fingertip-selection changes still invalidate setup. Experimental absolute pointing retains its stricter existing hand-generation reset behavior.

Results separate held-out error, raw jitter and tested coverage from training. If all five check targets were recorded but accuracy failed, the preview shows the measured 95th-percentile error and the unchanged 3% limit, both measured against the pointing area's diagonal. Your setup remains available for retry, recalibration or the relative alternative; the playground still requires a passing independent check.

The playground shows an adaptively smoothed pointer, optional raw pointer and the measured held-out error area. Smoothing affects display only; independent checks use fresh, unsmoothed evidence. During brief uncertainty, a faded, dashed **Last seen pointer** may remain for up to 450 milliseconds. It does not count as fresh tracking or contribute calibration/check samples. Fresh estimates stop outside the supported area or when tracking is stale. Practice targets never activate application controls, select objects, manipulate artifacts, transfer content or advance a Voyage.

**Explore guided demo** is a camera-free walkthrough with visibly simulated joints, movement controls and example interruptions. Successful demo results establish the software flow only. This local correction awaits another physical trial and protected integration. Real webcam accuracy, lighting tolerance, left/right usability, exposure-to-visible response time and comfort remain unqualified.

Use **Stop session** or the return link to stop owned camera/worker activity and clear session calibration. Preview mirroring is deliberate and starts a fresh session. Normal focused tracking defaults to a ceiling of 15 updates per second. Active, unpaused calibration collection and independent checks can request up to 60; pausing, preparation, results and the pointer playground use the normal ceiling. A visible page without focus is capped at 8. The model ceiling defaults to 60; effective scheduling uses the lower applicable phase/model limits. Administrators can set lower global limits. Available camera frames and processing pressure can reduce those rates further. Raising the ceiling recovers gradually, with one frame in flight and no queue; it does not promise 60 fps. Backgrounding or hiding the page stops camera activity and releases image buffers; enable it again when ready. Calibration is local to the session and expires after thirty minutes.

The standard laboratory's closed **Session diagnostics & privacy** drawer offers aggregate software timing and explicit download consent. Capture-to-canvas timing measures actual overlay submissions and excludes sensor exposure and display scanout; repeated unchanged drawings are skipped. The branch preview tries verified GPU processing when supported and falls back to the original CPU pipeline during initialization. Local diagnostics identify the actual processing path. A fresh full-image search accompanies bounded recent-region recovery; old landmarks never count as current evidence. Preparation may take up to 15 seconds under load. Capture requests an ideal 640×480, with image analysis capped at 320 pixels; actual camera output may differ. Smaller capture experiments increased fingertip error despite preserved detection counts. Aggregate downloads contain no footage, landmarks, hand/camera identifiers or private fit coefficients. **Delete calibration** clears the fit and aggregate trials while leaving the camera available; stopping clears the session.

A returning full hand takes gesture authority after three agreeing fresh frames spanning at least 120 ms. One wrong-digit return cannot replace the selected finger or create a persistent posture pause. When a coherent full hand is visible, its gesture evidence takes priority. An open or bent hand, multiple ambiguous hands, or disagreement about the selected index pauses collection. An image hint cannot override visible contrary full-hand evidence. When the hand is cropped, the same index previously associated with the image track can continue after several fresh, coherent observations of its four joints; the wrist and palm are not required. This partial view cannot start model-only tracking from scratch or clear a retained contrary gesture. If the model is unavailable, a supported fingertip image fallback can continue. These paths belong to one verified fingertip identity and calibration profile. A gray dashed ring means its image is still matched while pointing eligibility is paused; it is never used as a calibration sample or action. A pause keeps completed targets; selecting a new fingertip requires fresh setup.

A compatible full hand can support a substantial appearance change after several fresh pointing frames with coherent anatomy and motion from the same source. Image-only appearance changes still need a trustworthy match. Manually selecting a fingertip reserves image-only tracking; it cannot silently turn into model-only tracking after a loss.

The diagnostics drawer also separates measured capture problems from possible causes. Sustained stale results, processing pressure, rejected observations and model acquisition gaps have different labels. A poor-lighting or motion explanation is a hypothesis, not a certainty. A regular camera is the baseline; extra sensors are optional and must actually be available through the capture provider.

Where the current camera reports a reversible exposure control, Reach can try a bounded adjustment on that same camera. It keeps the change only if usable tracking improves while capture cadence and safety checks remain acceptable. Failed or unhelpful trials restore the previous setting and back off. A restoration failure stops the session with a persistent reason. This does not turn on an inferred IR light, require a second camera or guarantee recognition in darkness. If neither the hand model nor a selected fingertip supplies a trustworthy image region, Reach cannot tell whether an empty result means poor light or no pointing hand.

An appearance-reference pause means the selected fingertip can no longer be located consistently. Keep that fingertip visible and return to its earlier pose briefly. A clear image match can retain its identity during uncertain hand-model results; collection resumes only after fresh pointing and position evidence return. Diagnostics shows the current tracking decision separately from the number of detected hands. If its appearance stays substantially changed, mark it again for a fresh setup. Completed targets survive a temporary pause; a new fingertip selection cannot inherit the old calibration.

After you mark a fingertip, a failed first image or expired image track does not silently switch to a model-selected digit. Mark the visible fingertip again, or choose **Use automatic fingertip selection** explicitly. Both choices begin a fresh setup and explain why; the camera does not restart. A dashed image match remains display-only while full-hand pointing evidence disagrees.

## Availability and numeric reporting

Reach uses the platform's current global policy. If it is disabled or the policy cannot be refreshed safely, the laboratory cannot keep the camera running under an old local preference. There is no permanent client override. Loading/unavailable policy feedback is separate from tracking or gesture feedback.

When Reach and reporting are enabled by global policy, the laboratory automatically sends numeric aggregates: counts, durations, fixed timing/error bins, observed rates, actual handedness scores and calibration-check outcomes. Reports contain no footage, landmarks, fingertip/cursor positions or trajectories, private calibration coefficients, account/camera/hand/session identifiers, exact event timestamps, user-agent strings or free text. The collector keeps bounded aggregate windows in memory. Each report has an unrelated random retry key, with no persistent client identifier or account association. Reporting follows global policy and has no per-user setting.

Admiralty reports count windows rather than unique people or sessions. The server's retention setting defaults to 30 days, with a permitted 7–90 day range; expired reports are excluded and purged during report/policy access. Reporting can be disabled globally. Automatic numeric reporting never uploads local diagnostic video or trace downloads. MediaPipe's handedness score describes left/right classification, not confidence that a person is pointing; the current classifier supplies no numeric posture confidence.

These reports describe client-reported software behavior. They do not certify physical hand recognition, exposure-to-display latency, owner acceptance or a passing independent calibration. Timing percentiles pool fixed bins with missing measurements identified; the distribution of per-check P95 summaries is kept separate from pooled raw validation errors.

## Explicit local diagnostic recording

A separately enabled local testing page at `/devices/reach/record` is an explicit exception to the standard laboratory's no-recording behavior. It is available only on a loopback host in a developer-enabled preview. Choose **Enable camera preview**, then **Start guided video + trace recording**. Previewing alone does not start recording.

The 74-second guide gives each of nine pointing positions its own hold, followed by relaxed pointing, a continuous sweep, an open palm, a brief hide and recovery. It fits no calibration. By default, the diagnostic video saves the camera image and visible background without baking in hand landmarks; the live preview still shows them. Uncheck **Save camera image without hand overlay** before recording to retain the older overlaid format. Both formats are mirrored, re-encoded video with synchronized prompts below the camera region and no audio. The accompanying separate trace includes joint coordinates, inferred relative depth, model identity, timing and prompt labels. Those labels describe requested actions, not verified pointing intent or physical accuracy.

Use **Pause recording**, **Stop & review** or **Cancel & delete recording** as needed. Review the result, then manually choose **Download diagnostic video** and **Download landmark trace** before leaving. Leaving discards unsaved recordings and stops the camera. Nothing is uploaded, saved to an account or included in ordinary aggregate exports. Keep both downloads private and outside the repository; recordings must not be added to commits automatically. Local replay is diagnostic evidence, not physical accuracy qualification.
